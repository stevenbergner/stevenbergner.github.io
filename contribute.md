<p class="direction-lede">I am interested in work that leaves people and institutions more capable than it found them. This is a map of the topics and communities I hope to benefit, not a project-status report or a claim of institutional endorsement.</p>

<div class="direction-principle">
  <span>Working principle</span>
  <p>Technical progress is most useful when evidence remains inspectable, expertise stays connected to consequential decisions, and people can carry the resulting capability forward.</p>
</div>

## Evidence-aware AI

AI systems should make it easier to inspect evidence, uncertainty, and the path from source material to a conclusion. I am particularly interested in language and evidence interfaces for research and clinical work, where a useful answer must remain distinguishable from a confident one.

**People and systems I hope this benefits:** researchers, clinicians, reviewers, public-interest technologists, and communities affected by AI-supported decisions.

## Learning that becomes capability

Teaching, supervision, and technical mentorship can produce more than a completed course or task. They can create people who are able to lead projects, improve methods, teach others, and build durable infrastructure. I want to develop learning materials and practice instruments that make this transfer visible and repeatable.

**People and systems I hope this benefits:** students, co-op participants, research assistants, early-career developers, instructors, and interdisciplinary teams.

## Faculty-led interdisciplinary research

Research increasingly crosses academic units, operational teams, shared computing infrastructure, public agencies, and industry partners. Those collaborations work best when each role contributes its knowledge without erasing academic lineage, technical provenance, or the authority needed to interpret the work responsibly.

**People and systems I hope this benefits:** faculty building new programmes, technical research staff, project and research managers, shared-infrastructure teams, and external collaborators.

## Decisions that preserve expertise

Budgets, delivery constraints, technical evidence, partner obligations, scientific value, and human effects can all be relevant to one decision. I am exploring simple instruments that show what was considered, who was qualified to interpret it, who decided, and how an error can be corrected.

**People and systems I hope this benefits:** universities, public institutions, research organizations, and teams making consequential decisions across professional boundaries.

## Reproducible research and responsible handover

Code, data transformations, model configurations, contribution histories, and review decisions should survive personnel and infrastructure changes. Reproducibility is not only a technical property; it is also a way to support collaboration, correction, credit, and continuity.

**People and systems I hope this benefits:** authors, students, maintainers, research partners, and future teams inheriting work they did not originally create.
